Community Project FAQ

1544 North Colonial Terrace, Arlington, VA

Highgate's Owners Association is beginning a waterproofing and terrace replacement project affecting a separate section of the community. Below are answers to the questions buyers ask most, in plain language.

Q.What's happening in the Highgate community?
The HOA is replacing the waterproofing system and expansion joints beneath the terraces at the 1501–1507 Colonial Terrace units, addressing water intrusion protection at those homes. The work is being done in two phases:
Aug 3, 2026Tentative construction start — Phase 1 (units 1505–1507)
~6–7 monthsPhase 1 duration, followed directly by Phase 2 (units 1501–1503)
Late 2027Estimated completion of both phases (project spans just over a year total)
During the initial 3–4 week demolition period, residents throughout Highgate may notice some added noise, dust, and construction traffic in the community — typical for any active HOA capital project — but all work, equipment, and access changes are confined to the 1501–1507 units.
Q.Does this affect 1544 N Colonial Terrace?
No construction at this home
Physical work is limited to the 1501–1507 units — a different, separate section of the community. No construction, access changes, or disruption will occur at 1544 N Colonial Terrace. Because the terraces are Common Area maintained by the Association, all 109 Highgate units — including this one — share in the special assessment that funds the work. As explained below, that assessment is being fully satisfied by the seller at closing.
Q.Why is this project happening?
Highgate was built between 1991 and 1993, making the community roughly 33–35 years old — the age at which original terrace waterproofing membranes and expansion joints on townhomes of this era commonly reach the end of their service life. The HOA's resale disclosure certificate confirms the assessment funds repair and replacement of the waterproofing membrane and related improvements on the community's Common Area.
Q.What does this cost the buyer?
No cost to the buyer — paid off in full at closing
Per the HOA Resale Disclosure Certificate:
  • Total special assessment: $16,800 per unit, running 1/1/2026–10/1/2027, payable as a lump sum or in $2,100 quarterly installments.
  • Current balance on this unit: $12,600.
  • HOA rule: the full remaining balance becomes due immediately if a home sells during the assessment period.
Because of that rule, the seller is required to pay off the entire remaining balance at settlement. The buyer takes title with the assessment fully satisfied — no balance, no future installments, no cost.
